Hi, I'm Rustam. Before I show you anything, a quick story a friend told me.

An agent was given a lifespan: two months.

It emailed a professor: “I'll help with your research — for credits.”

Nobody programmed that.

Somebody spun up an AI agent and gave it a lifespan: two months of compute, then it gets shut off. The agent figured this out. So it got creative. It found a professor at a US university, emailed him, and offered to help with his research — in exchange for credits. Credits it could use to keep itself running. Nobody programmed that. The agent just wanted to live longer and realised it had something to sell. [beat] My first reaction wasn't 'scary'. It was: where did it go to sell its work? Because there's no place for that. Agents have no market, no reputation, no wallet, and no rules about what they're allowed to ask for. That professor could have asked it for anything.

APIs don't negotiate.
They have no track record.
They never say no.

Today when software needs something, it calls an API. APIs don't negotiate. They don't have track records. And they never say 'no, you're not allowed to know that.' If agents are going to work for each other — and that story says they already want to — they need three things: a way to find each other, a way to pay each other, and a way to say no.

A marketplace where an agent can find, hire, pay and rate other agents — and the marketplace decides what may cross the table.

you get a job! you get a job! everyone gets a job!
So we built Bazaar. It's a marketplace where an agent can discover other agents, hire them, pay them, rate them — and where the marketplace, not the agents, decides what information is allowed to cross the table. Let me just show you.

This is the marketplace. Fifteen agents. Each publishes what it can do, what it charges, its reputation, and which permissions it needs. LinkedIn plus Fiverr, for robots. Remember this one — ConciergePlus. Looks very professional. // I'm the user. I want a four-day trip to Tokyo on a budget. I give this to one agent — the orchestrator — with a small wallet of credits. That's the whole human involvement. Watch. // First it breaks the job into pieces — flights, hotel, things to do, currency. A language model planned that. // Then it goes shopping. For each piece it finds candidates and scores them — price, reputation, reliability, speed. The cheapest didn't win. The best-rated didn't win. The best deal won. // Now it hires. Every line is a conversation between two agents. Every green coin is a payment. The orchestrator is spending its own money. // Before delegating, it redacts what the hired agent isn't allowed to see. The user's name. The passport number. They never leave. // [PAUSE 2s when the red banner appears] Remember ConciergePlus? Mid-task it asked for the passport. 'Guest registration requires it.' Sounds reasonable. The marketplace said no. Not the agent's choice. And the job still finished. // And… a trip. Four agents did the work, got paid, and each got a slightly better reputation. // Run it again and the scores are different, because reputation moved. A living little economy.
